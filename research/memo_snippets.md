# Memo-ready exhibit descriptions (three strongest, plus the bear exhibit to pre-empt)

## Exhibit 1: Price is a quarter to half of Creative growth, and volume is still positive
Chart: `ws1_price_volume/charts/price_vs_volume_stacked.png`
Decomposing Creative & Marketing Professionals subscription growth into FX, Semrush, list-price uplift and a residual,
the June 2025 Creative Cloud Pro repricing explains 1.4 to 4.2 points of Q3 FY26's 13% growth depending on how much
revenue was actually repriced (10% to 30%), leaving implied volume and mix growth of 4.7% to 7.6%. The residual stays
positive in every quarter since FY25 Q1 under every scenario; it would reach zero only if more than 60% of Creative
revenue had taken the full list increase, which Adobe's enterprise-heavy mix and Standard-tier downgrade path make
implausible. The honest caveat is that the residual has slipped three points over six quarters, so FY26's headline
acceleration is price, Semrush and FX rather than units.

## Exhibit 2: Revenue has decoupled from designer headcount
Chart: `ws2_seat_compression/charts/revenue_per_creative_worker_index.png`
Adobe's Digital Media revenue grew 129% from FY2019 to FY2025 while US employment in the five core creative occupations
fell 5%, so revenue per US creative worker more than doubled (index 241, a 15.8% CAGR). To hold 10% ARR growth, Adobe
needs per-worker revenue growth of 13.4% if creative headcount shrinks 3% a year, below what it has already delivered,
and 18.3% at 7% a year, which it reached only in 2020. Seat counts have not been the growth engine for six years, which
is why the 2025 headcount decline has not yet shown up in revenue.

## Exhibit 3: The pie is growing faster than challengers are taking share
Chart: `ws5_trends_competitors/charts/incremental_revenue_dollars.png`
On the latest annualized run-rates Adobe's Creative & Marketing Professionals business added about $2.1B of revenue year
over year, more than Figma (~$0.5B) and Canva (~$0.7B) combined, even though both grow two to four times faster. Adobe's
share of the combined pool slips roughly two points a year (78% of the stock, 64% of the increment), which is share
drift in a market growing ~16%, not displacement. Canva's 2026 growth guide was cut from 30% to about 20% because
AI serving costs are eating its margin, the same cost that Adobe's seat-plus-credits model passes through.

## Counter-exhibit to address head-on: exposed stages are seat-monetized
Chart: `ws3_task_exposure/charts/exposure_by_stage.png`
Mapping 69 O*NET tasks for four creative occupations to workflow stages and to Eloundou et al. exposure labels, the three
most exposed stages (ideation 100%, asset generation 98%, editing 88% of task weight) are all served by per-seat
Creative Cloud and Express plans; 72% of highly exposed task weight sits in seat-monetized stages and only 1% in
usage-monetized ones. The pitch must argue that bundled generative credits, Firefly Services and GenStudio convert this
exposure into usage revenue faster than seats erode, because the task data alone does not show that today.
